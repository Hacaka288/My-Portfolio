How I Work

I don't have a fixed technological identity. The tools change, I adapt. When I need to build something, I learn what I need, choose the appropriate tools, build the system, find the problems, and adjust.

01

Beyond the Screen

I've built and operated physical products. That changes how you think about software. A product isn't finished because the code works. Distribution and operations are part of the build.

02

Stack Agnostic

Whatever tool or framework best solves the problem is what I use. The value isn't in being able to name technologies. It's in what you can build with them.

03

End-to-End Delivery

From backend logic and real-time state to production-ready interfaces and payment infrastructure. I handle full product lifecycles without unnecessary handoffs.

04

Systems First

The interesting part is never any individual feature. It's making all the parts behave like one system: consistent, predictable, and coherent under pressure.

Background

The common thread isn't a particular framework. It's the ability to move from "this should exist" to "this exists."

Computers fascinated me before I could articulate why. Growing up, I watched my family trade forex (charts, levels, position sizing) and something about the idea that markets were just information systems with exploitable structure stuck with me early. My mom got me a PC, eventually a laptop, and I spent most of that time taking things apart and figuring out how they worked.

I've been building software and freelancing since around 2022. I studied Software Engineering at Babcock University, though most of my actual development happened outside the classroom: building, breaking, researching, rebuilding, and shipping.

I've worked across frontend development, backend systems, real-time applications, payment infrastructure, databases, product interfaces, automation, scraping, and AI-related work. That range isn't accidental. I've always moved toward whatever problem was most interesting, regardless of whether I already knew the tools.

The financial side never fully left. I've studied forex and quantitative finance, taken prop firm challenges, and spent time on strategies around mean reversion, cross-market arbitrage between betting platforms, and other positive expected-value opportunities where information asymmetry or mispricing creates an edge. I sit at 1300 Elo in chess on Skillest, which is roughly how I approach most problems: find the structure, find the edge, don't overextend.

I'm interested in systems that make people more capable: financial infrastructure, digital economies, business automation, and tools that turn complicated information into something people can actually act on.

Outside engineering, I do graphics design, draw, edit in After Effects, and do mobile UI work. Creative and technical thinking are the same muscle used differently.
